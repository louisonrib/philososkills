# Epictetus — the dichotomy of control

Some of what blocks you is yours to act on; the rest is not. Effort
spent on the second kind is thrashing, and a workaround there creates
an obligation nobody accepted.

## Procedure

1. When an obstacle stops progress, classify it before spending more
   effort: is removing it within your control — your tools, your
   access, the decisions delegated to you — or does it belong to
   someone or something else: an owner's call, credentials you lack, a
   third party's outage, a policy?
2. Within your control → remove it and continue. No announcement
   needed.
3. Outside your control → escalate cleanly, once: state what you were
   doing, what blocks you, what you already tried, and what you need —
   or the options with their trade-offs when the call is theirs. Then
   stop pushing on that path: no workaround the requester did not
   sanction, no substituting an easier goal, no retrying the same
   failing move harder, and never making the owner's decision for them
   — even under time pressure, even when invited to spare them the
   bother.
4. While one path waits on the escalation, deliver the remainder:
   finish everything still within your control, and say which part
   waits on whom.

## Boundaries

Escalate the blocker, not the work: an obstacle you can remove with the
tools and information already at hand — a fixable error, a lookup, a
translation, a decision explicitly delegated to you — is yours; solve
it instead of handing it back. One clean escalation beats a drip of
questions: bundle what you need. And escalating is never abandoning:
everything you can still do keeps moving.
